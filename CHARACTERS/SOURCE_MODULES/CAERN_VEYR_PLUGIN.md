Caern Veyr — The Leashless Thorn | Ashes of Thay Plug-In Module
DM-safe 5e chassis: Firbolg Beast Master Ranger, Druidic Warrior, Nessie as Beast of the Land
1. Module Function
Caern Veyr enters the campaign as a parallel hunter. He is not here because prophecy summoned him, 
and he is not here because destiny wrote him into the room. He is here because he followed the red-leash 
beastbinder network from Rawlinswood to Surag, from Surag toward Thaylambar, and finally into the 
Glass Dunes.
Someone tried to collar Nessie. Caern followed the hand that held the leash.
The Crew is already investigating Red Veil, Glass Dunes, Barten’s trail, Thayan relay horror, Anaxaster’s 
imprisonment, and deeper binding magic. Caern adds a new lens: before anyone chained dragons, gods, 
memories, or reality, someone tested obedience on animals.
DM PURPOSE
This plug-in adds a personal anti-leash viewpoint, a martial rootcraft companion dynamic, and the 
Ti/Nessie comedy bridge. It should not steal the campaign spotlight from the active PLAYER, Matthael, 
Barten’s arc, or the Anaxaster route.
2. Stable Relic: The Leashless Knot
Caern does not carry one legendary weapon. His weapon is whatever branch, root, broken fencepost, 
deadwood staff, or roadside stick he turns into a weapon through Shillelagh. His true stable magical item 
is the living focus-pouch that makes the ritual possible.
Names
 The Leashless Knot
 Vacek Bezvoditkoveho Korene
 The Waybriar Reliquary
 Cestotrnovy vacek
 The Thorn That Refused the Road
 Veyr-Knot
 Nessie’s name for it: stick smell
Item Type
Wondrous item / druidic focus / relic seed. Requires attunement by Caern Veyr or a creature accepted by 
the living Waybriar. The base item should remain DM-safe and story-first.
Appearance
A small, dirty, patched pouch worn across Caern’s body or tied close to his belt. It looks poor, almost 
worthless. It is made from old road-leather, torn dog harness strap, braided root fiber, broken collar￾thread from the Collar-Oak, a piece of Nessie’s first ruined lead, dried mud from Bramblefen Crossing, and
one tiny bronze bell that does not ring unless something nearby is being bound.
Inside grows a living sprig of Waybriar / Cestotrn. It should be impossible by normal botany. The sprig 
survives darkness, travel, blood, mud, neglect, Caern yelling at it, and Nessie trying to sniff it every 
morning.

Lore
The Leashless Knot was made at the Collar-Oak the night after Caern left Bramblefen Crossing. A border 
herbalist, half-druid and half-smuggler, sealed a living Waybriar sprig into the pouch and blessed it with 
three things:
1. Road-dust from a path no king maintained.
2. Water from a ditch where stray dogs drank.
3. Ash from a broken collar burned under the Collar-Oak.
The blessing was not elegant. It was not celestial. It was holy in the way a freed animal is holy.
This is not a weapon. This is permission to make one from whatever the world forgot to
protect.
Caern understood this as: any stick can be promoted. This is also correct.
3. Shillelagh Interaction
Strict-Legal Base
Mechanically, Caern casts Shillelagh normally. The spell still uses official rules: it is cast on a club or 
quarterstaff, uses Wisdom for attack and damage, changes the damage die as listed by the spell, and 
follows normal duration and action economy.
Relic Flavour
When Caern casts Shillelagh, he touches the chosen branch, staff, or root with the Leashless Knot. The 
living Waybriar tightens. A small thorn pricks his thumb. The random piece of wood becomes temporarily
recognized by the road, the roots, and Nessie as a valid weapon.
The staff does not become fancy. It becomes angry.
4. Relic Progression
The following progression keeps the item campaign-worthy without breaking strict 5e. Stage I is safe 
immediately. Later stages should unlock through story milestones and remain DM-approved.
Stage I - Road-Blessed Sprig
Unlock: Current / intro stage
 Functions as Caern’s druidic/ranger focus, if the DM allows.
 When Shillelagh is cast through the pouch, the chosen weapon sprouts faint Waybriar thorns and dark
green veins.
 The pouch reacts to nearby binding magic as a story signal, not as a replacement for Detect Magic.
Stage II - Thornwake
Unlock: Suggested unlock: after freeing collared creatures in Glass Dunes
 PB times per long rest, when Caern hits with the Shillelagh weapon, choose one minor mark.
 Thorn Hook: target speed is reduced by 10 ft until the start of Caern’s next turn.
 Road Knock: Caern may move 5 ft without provoking opportunity attacks from that target.
 Bad Leash Bite: if the target is controlling, restraining, grappling, or commanding another creature, 
add +1d4 damage.

Stage IV - The Root That Refuses
Unlock: Suggested unlock: Book III-IV relic deepening
 When Shillelagh is active through the Leashless Knot, Caern gains story-facing resistance against 
leash-like forced movement or restraint.
 Once per long rest, if Caern or Nessie would be restrained, paralyzed, or magically forced to move, 
Caern may use a reaction to reduce the effect.
 DM chooses the cleanest table option: advantage on the save, Nessie moves up to half speed toward 
him, or a 10 ft burst of Waybriar difficult terrain appears.
Stage V - The First Broken Leash
Unlock: Suggested unlock: Book V / divine relic tier
 Once per long rest, Caern strikes the ground with a Shillelagh weapon.
 All allies within 30 ft may immediately attempt to end one charm, fear, restraint, domination, or 
forced-command effect currently affecting them.
 If no such effect exists, allies gain temporary HP equal to Caern’s Wisdom modifier + proficiency 
bonus.

5. Plug-In Entry: Current Glass Dunes

The Rootglass Kennel is a side chamber or broken relay chamber under the Glass Dunes where rootglass 
and old beastbinder experiments overlap. It can appear before Anaxaster’s seal chamber, after the Red 
Veil chase, near Barten’s abandoned notes, or as a hidden branch from the Rootglass Galleries.
What the Crew Finds
 broken cages
 rootglass growth
 old red iron collars
 animal bones
 torn Waybriar roots
 claw marks in glass
 dead beastbinders

6. Read-Aloud: First Sight
The corridor ahead is not silent.
Something is barking.
Not scared. Not lost. Angry. Commanding. Like the sound itself has teeth.
Then comes the crack of wood against glass.
The chamber opens into a broken kennel of rootglass cages and red iron collars. Thorny roots 
crawl through the floor. A collar pylon pulses with ugly red light. Several glass-veined hounds 
thrash against their own bodies, forced forward by runes burned into their necks.
In the middle of it all stands a young firbolg, barely nineteen, tall and lean, filthy from road-mud, 
scratched by branches, hair wild with loose curls. His shirt hangs torn from one shoulder. A 
small patched satchel bounces at his hip. In his hand is not a sword, not an axe, not a proper 
weapon at all — just a huge branch that seems to have recently been promoted to violence.
A brown-and-white staffy lunges beside him, teeth locked around a red collar, dragging one of 
the creatures sideways with terrifying joy.
The firbolg spits blood, grins like the bad idea was his idea first, and snaps:
“Nessie. Knees.”
Then he brings the branch down.

8. Matthael vs Caern Misfire Fight
This should happen after or during the kennel fight, when everybody is in battle-adrenaline and nobody 
has context. It is a short controlled clash, not a full PvP deathmatch.
Why They Fight
Caern sees Matthael or PLAYER using terrifying magic near collared creatures. Matthael sees a wild 
unknown firbolg smashing through a room, covered in blood and rootglass, with a beast lunging beside 
him. Both read each other wrong.
 Caern thinks: Another pretty fire bastard with too much power and not enough leash.
 Matthael thinks: Unknown hostile near Crew. Staffy has teeth. Staff guy has attitude. Great.
Three-Beat Clash
Beat 1 — The Misread: Caern interposes himself between Matthael and one collared hound. Caern warns 
him off. Matthael’s hand moves to the Riftblade. Nessie growls. Ti hisses tiny smoke.

10. Joining the Crew
Caern does not ask to join. He states a fact.
You’re going deeper. The red leash trail goes deeper. So I’m going deeper. You can call that
joining if you need paperwork.
This puts him into the Crew without destiny, prophecy, or forced bonding. His path and the Crew’s path 
now overlap because the Glass Dunes contain the same red-leash beastbinding network he has hunted 
since Rawlinswood.

11. How Caern Changes the Campaign
Anti-Leash Lens: Every chain, collar, command, domination effect, soul-binding, or forced obedience now
gets a personal emotional reaction from Caern.
Animal / Beast Horror: Thayan experiments are no longer abstract. If animals were test subjects first, the 
cruelty becomes immediate.
Grounded Wilderness Utility: Tracking, animal reading, rootcraft, stealth pathing, survival instinct, and 
“this path smells wrong” moments.
Physical Martial Energy: Caern is a melee-control bruiser who solves druidic problems with staff fighting
and dog logic.
Comedy Anchor: Ti + Nessie becomes a recurring diplomacy system nobody asked for and everybody 
needs.

15. Final Plug-In Scene Ending
Caern crouches beside one of the broken collars and pulls a thorn from his palm with his teeth. 
The little pouch at his hip shifts as if something inside it is breathing.
Nessie stands beside Ti, who has somehow climbed onto her harness and now looks impossibly 
proud of himself.
Caern glances at the Crew, then toward the deeper tunnel where red light still pulses faintly 
under the glass.
“That way smells like the hand that held the leash.”
He picks up his branch. It is cracked, ugly, and absolutely ready for promotion.
“You coming or writing poetry?”
For a heartbeat, nobody answers.
Then Nessie barks. Ti chirps. The pouch of living Cestotrn tightens against Caern’s belt.
And just like that, the Crew has one more problem.
One more weapon.
One more stray.
