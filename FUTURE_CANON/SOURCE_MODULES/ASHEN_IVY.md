Ashen Ivy Module
Panaeolo-Cineris IX / Netherleaf / “Hero Grade”
Type: Lore herb, consumable, investigation object, roleplay amplifier
Recommended level: 7–11
Main themes: Weave-attunement, pain relief, Thayan corruption, Silverstrain 
foreshadowing, Matthael being absolutely normal about a plant.
Do not reveal yet: Ashen Ivy contains micro-traces of wounded 
Spellfire/Silverstrain resonance.
1. Core Lore
Panaeolo was once a rare Netherese herb cultivated near places where the 
Weave ran close to the surface. It was not “a drug” in the ordinary sense. It was
a Weave-attunement plant: mages used it to sharpen spell-feel, deepen 
concentration, and temporarily perceive the texture of magic.
After Netheril fell, wild panaeolo survived in ruins, buried gardens, cracked 
arcanist vaults, and Anauroch sink-sites.
The Red Wizards of Thay later recovered it.
They did what Thay does best:
They ruined it professionally.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 2 / 29>
Through ash-binding, pain extraction, chain-scar exposure, and failed 
containment rituals, they created a corrupted strain:
Ashen Ivy
A grey-green living herb with black veins of sorrow and bitterness and a silver￾white limning along its edges. It looks burned, but it is alive. It does not glow 
like fire. It gleams like something that survived fire and now remembers it 
personally.
2. Appearance
Living Panaeolo
Fresh panaeolo is a curling vine-herb with leathery green leaves, pale veins, 
and delicate grasping stems. It smells faintly of rain on old stone, parchment, 
and crushed green stems.
Dried Panaeolo
Muted green, tan, and olive leaves. Slightly leathery. Arcane smell, but clean.
Living Ashen Ivy
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 3 / 29>
Ash-grey and green leaves. Black branching veins. Silver-white edges. Charcoal 
stems with faint violet undertones. It looks mournful, expensive, and like it 
knows your worst memory but is politely not saying it yet.
Dried Ashen Ivy
Dark green-grey shredded leaf, blackened vein fragments, silver-white flecks. 
When crushed, it releases a soft cold smoke smell: bitter herbs, old ash, frost, 
and distant lightning.
3. In-Game Explanation Scene
Use this when the Crew first properly identifies it.
Read-Aloud
The dried leaves do not crumble like normal herbs.
They fold.
Even dead, they keep a little shape, like they are pretending to still be alive.
Eryn’s fingers hover over the dark green-grey flakes. His face tightens.
“Not native,” he says. “Not anymore.”
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 4 / 29>
Norbyn leans closer, squinting. “That’s not just ash.”
The black veins in the leaf pieces catch the light. Not red. Not ember. Something
colder. Silver-white lines glimmer across the edges, thin as knife cuts.
Barten goes very quiet.
Mira, from the doorway, mutters, “Anything that pretty and that sad is either 
poison or expensive.”
The smoke rising from the bowl bends toward Matthael’s scarred arm.
Not toward the air.
Toward the wound.
Ti sniffs it once.
Then sneezes a tiny puff of silver-black smoke and looks personally offended.

5. Consumable Mechanics
Ashen Ivy — Standard Dose
Use: Smoked, smoldered, infused, or alchemically burned.
Duration: 1 hour.
Safe uses: 1 dose per long rest.
Risk begins: second dose before long rest.
Benefit
Choose one when used:
A. Quiet the Scar
For 1 hour, gain advantage on one saving throw against fear, pain, forced 
memory, chain-scar interference, or psychic distress.
B. Weave Feel
For 1 hour, gain +1d4 to one Arcana check, spell-control check, ritual check, or 
magical investigation roll.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 7 / 29>
C. Frostfire Channel — Matthael Only
Once within 1 hour, when casting a cold, fire, force, or smoke-flavored spell, 
Matthael may add one of these effects:
Describe the spell through frostfire-smoke and gain +1 damage per spell level,
Reroll one damage die,
Detect whether the target has chain, Thayan, or fracture resonance.
Tiny bonus, huge flavor. Like seasoning, but for trauma.
6. Social Side Effect
For the duration, the user becomes more honest, intense, emotionally exposed, 
or strange.
Choose one:
Effect Mechanic
Cracked Honesty Disadvantage on one Deception check.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 8 / 29>
Too Intense -1d4 to one Persuasion check where calm diplomacy matters.
Beautiful Catastrophe +1d4 to one Intimidation or Performance check when 
dramatic presence matters.
Haunted Clarity Advantage on one Insight check involving pain, grief, 
addiction, magical trauma, or manipulation.
For Matthael, this should not feel like “he is high.”
It should feel like the mask stopped holding for a while.

8. Quest Hook: “The Garden That Burned Alive”
Purpose
Explain Ashen Ivy in-game and create a route toward a stronger, safer version.
Hook
A black-market herbalist claims to have “true Netherleaf,” but the sample 
reacts violently to Matthael’s scars. The Crew traces it to a buried Netherese 
greenhouse beneath a ruined Thayan holding site.
Locations
1. Smuggler’s Pouch
Contains poor-quality Ashen Ivy mixed with ash, dried nettle, and powdered 
bone.
DC 14 Medicine/Nature: unsafe cut.
DC 16 Arcana: still contains real panaeolo-derived matter.
2. Thayan Drying Room
Hooks, copper trays, ash bowls, pain-extraction glyphs.
Encounter Options:
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 11 / 29>
2–4 Thayan residue-wights,
Animated chain-hooks,
One herbal alchemy hazard cloud.
3. Netherese Root Vault
Ancient growth chamber. Broken sunlight mirrors. Dead irrigation spells. One 
living panaeolo root still growing through cracked silver-black stone.
The Ashen Ivy here is not harvested.
It is mourning.

10.Hero Grade Ashen Ivy
Rare consumable. Matthael-attuned.
When used, choose one:
End one frightened condition on yourself.
Gain advantage on one save against Chain magic.
When casting a spell this scene, turn its visual expression into silver-edged 
frostfire smoke and gain +1d4 to one damage roll or spell-control roll.
Ask the DM one sensory question: “What does the Weave feel like here?”
Side effect: Matthael must say something emotionally honest, stupidly brave, or
both.
Example:
 “I’m fine. That’s a technical term meaning I’m standing and nobody has 
stabbed me successfully enough yet.”
